The business today

An operating, profitable online retail business

CPB Distribution is a UK-registered online retail business based in Newcastle upon Tyne. Since trading began in September 2024, the business has bought and sold products across a wide range of categories and well-known brands — including Febreze, Corsair, GoPro, Optimum Nutrition and Ninja — building a strong, self-funded trading track record.

Every quarter since launch, revenue has grown — and rather than draw profit out of the business, it has all been reinvested into stock and working capital. That's built a genuine, self-funded growth trajectory without any external investment to date.

The growth model

Why the model works

Growth so far hasn't depended on outside capital — it's come from a repeatable cycle that compounds every quarter.

01

Buy smart

Source well-known branded stock and list it competitively, building sales velocity and category knowledge across our retail channels.

02

Sell Online

List and move stock through established online retail channels, taking advantage of their reach, trust, and fulfilment infrastructure.

03

Reinvest fully

Profit goes straight back into more stock and working capital — funding the next, larger cycle of buying and selling.

Where investment comes in

The opportunity: accelerating the move to wholesale

Our online retail sales have proven the model and funded steady growth — but wholesale is where the business can scale meaningfully further. Wholesale buying needs more up-front capital per order than our current retail sourcing, but delivers better margins, more predictable stock, and stronger long-term brand relationships.

We're opening conversations with investors who want to help fund larger wholesale purchase orders and speed up the relationships we're already building with brands and distributors — putting growth on a faster track than reinvested profit alone can achieve.

What investment would be used for

  • Funding larger, bulk wholesale purchase orders
  • Securing better terms and volume discounts with suppliers
  • Expanding into new product categories and brands
  • Building stock buffers to support consistent retail availability
  • Strengthening working capital as order volumes grow
